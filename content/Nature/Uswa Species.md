There is one intelligent species on the continent, called the Uswa. A quasi-reptile species, they are highly adaptable, able to biologically adapt to their environments in a matter of generations.

## Name

The name 'Uswa' is the common name for the species, or more specifically, people or all non-animal beings. It is derived from 'ū-Sunahua', meaning 'Many-those that Ripple/Wave'

## Form

##### Body

Usw are bipedal, with flexed digitigrade legs, meaning they walk with their heels raised. They are capable of running at greater speeds than humans, at the cost of stability. When standing still or at rest, members species can shift to a plantigrade position for comfort.

They have two arms, which are longer than a human's, extending to the knee whilst standing. Each hand has five digits, with an opposable thumb. Each digit has a soft round pad at the end, with a short claw.

The species has tails that they once used for balance, but these have gradually shortened over millennia as they adjusted to walking upright. Tail length varies greatly, and some cultures even ritually amputate the rail, seeing it as uncivilised.

##### Build

Owing to the species' incredible ability to rapidly adapt to new environments, height, weight and build vary considerably. Those in colder highland climes adapt thicker, bulkier torsos to conserve heat, whilst those living in plains adapted more slender physiques to improve speed and agility.

##### Skin

Uswa 'skin' is in fact a series of tiny scales, ranging in colour from coppery orange, to yellow-gold, silver and grey, with many subtle variants besides. Far from just the appearance of metal, the keratinous scales have a high metal content, which generally aligns with their colour. Scales that have been peeled off or damaged have a tendency to return larger and harder, like scar tissue.

Skin colour is one of the main ways to tell an Uswa's age. Copper-skinned individuals turn from orange to green as they age, whilst the iron-skinned turn from grey to red, and the golden-skinned simply have their lustre fade.

##### Hair

Instead of hair, Uswa have metallic spines that grow along the top of their head. These always match their skin colour, but are generally darker. They can be styled, but are much coarser and harder than hair.

##### Eyes

Uswa are a predator species. Their eyes are front-facing and their pupils are round. They don’t have sclera, but have irises in a range of colours.  

## Biology

##### Temperature

Uswa are ectotherms. They can’t produce much heat internally, so inhabit mostly warmer regions that remain above freezing year-round.

##### Sex, Gender & Reproduction

All Uswa may both sire and bear children, and they do not have a concept of sex. In place of gender roles, they have a set of archetypes within kin groups, that are usually assigned at adulthood.

Uswa lay clutches of between three to ten eggs, which must be incubated for several months. Young are raised in larger family units with at least four 'parents'.

##### Lifespan & Metabolism

Uswa grow all of their life, though the speed and form of this growth varies greatly with age and body type. The species has a much greater range in body types that makes up for their lack of sexual dimorphism, and is tied to the seasons, or rather the temperature and conditions when an egg is laid.

Typically, older Uswa grow larger but more slender, losing muscle mass and flexibility, whilst young members of the species experience a huge burst of growth around sexual maturity.

The species has a natural lifespan much longer than humans. 70 years is considered middle age, and older Uswa at 100 or more are not unheard of, though few live past 130. This is partly due to the species' metabolism.

When needed, they can boost their metabolism for periods of extreme activity, where they eat more and have considerably more energy. These bursts are normally triggered by stress, and last for up to two months, allowing for great feats of survival and production. Afterwards, a period of 'brumation' is needed to recover, where the body *slows* down and consumes less energy, lasting around six months, before it returns to a more steady rhythm. All Uswa spend some portion of their lives in these three modes.
