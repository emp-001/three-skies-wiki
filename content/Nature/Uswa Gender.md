The Uswa have only one sex, or rather, most members of the species can both bear and sire children. As such, gender in their societies is not assigned based on sexual characteristics at birth, but is instead assigned based on how closely character and physical traits align to set roles. Like in our world, Uswa gender defines social & legal status, dress, professions and pronouns. The exact number of genders, as well as how they are assigned and the ability to change them, varies between cultures.

This article will focus on how gender is understood within the core nations of Three Skies.

Though the exact age and rituals vary, gender is typically assigned by older family members or community elders during a coming-of-age ceremony in early adulthood.

Fluidity and changing of gender is a religious matter. Followers of the True Faith commonly hold that gender is chosen by the Virtuous Aspects, and elders merely interpret their vision for an individual. As such, the process for changing ones gender is long and complex; comparable to securing a divorce in Medieval Europe on our world. Conforming to one's assigned gender is regarded as moral and proper.

This is by no means a universal framework. In Tuklua, where the grip of the Faith is relatively weak, individuals may change gender frequently, to fill roles within their clan. And in the frontier provinces of Three Skies, some cultures change genders to align with stars, seasons or simply the current needs of the community.

Broadly speaking, among cultures that allow it, it is easier to change to another gender within the physical (External to Internal) axis than to move outside it, because mind and personality are seen as less changeable than physique.

## Distribution

There is not an even distribution of genders. The numbers of any particular gender in a community can change according to current needs and issues.

In times of war, there may be a mass die-off of Dams and Tous, such that some cultures allow gender change to rebalance society.

## Reproduction

Because there are no sexes tied to siring or bearing children, these are seen simply as another gendered task.  
  
In Three Skies, strict norms define which genders may reproduce, and how.

Broadly, mind genders are expected to sire children, and body genders are expected to bear them, on account of being more physically able.

However, internality and externality also come into play here, leading to a unique situation for each gender.

- Koras may bear children on account of being an internal gender. On account of being a mind gender, they may also sire children.
- Kimas may only bear children on account of being a body gender, and are seen as the default for this role. They may not sire children.
- Dams may only sire children. They are seen as the head of the family, and all children carry their clan name, regardless of actual sire. To bear a child is seen as unfitting of their externality.
- Tous may neither bear nor sire children. Their body gender would otherwise require them to bear children, but as they are an external gender, they are often soldiers, labourers and hunters, roles where bearing a child would be seen as too risky.

The system in other cultures may be more or less restrictive, for example, all external genders siring and all internal genders bearing.

## The Family Unit

Couples as we understand them in our world are also rare, and broadly families with three or more partners are the norm.

In Three Skies, the ideal family consists of four members, with one representative from each gender. Smaller units do exist, but are seen as incomplete, especially if they lack a Dam, the traditional head. Larger units with more than one of each gender are permitted, but seen as unstable.

To speak of monogamy is impossible for the Uswa. It is more accurate to think of relationships as being open or closed; whether they permit outside partners. The difference is cultural and religious, but in regions that follow the True Faith, polycules are closed - members are bound together in a formal marriage, and relations outside of their unit are viewed as adulterous.  In other cultures, polycules may be open, or others can be formally invited.

Young are raised communally by the family unit, though most responsibility is placed upon Koras to nurse and educate them.

## Sexuality

Because anyone can bear a child, there are strict norms around sexual acts. These broadly align with who may or may not sire and bear children, at least within the scope of Three Skies. Specifically, this means Koras and Dams may be the active partner, Kimas may only ever be the passive partner, whilst Tous may not engage in sex at all - though most will find other ways to satisfy themselves.

How far this is respected within bonded groups varies greatly.

Sex before marriage is taboo in Three Skies, but this isn't true universally.

## Theory

It would be impossible to cover every possible gender dynamic on the continent. However, most cultures have more than two genders, which can be best understood as the confluence of two scales: a physical scale and a mental scale.

The physical scale is almost universal, and is 'External to Internal' whether the gender is focused on domestic tasks, or works mostly outside of the household. Broadly, those who are more physically powerful are seen as better suited to external genders. Often, this means the balance of power sits with external genders, but this is not always the case.

It is the mental scale that varies most between cultures. It is based on an assessment of an individual's personality, so is of course very subjective. Common mental scales are 'Passive to Active' 'Clean to Unclean', 'Individual to Collective' and 'Fast to Slow'.

So, for example, one culture's gender scales might be 'Domestic to External' and 'Slow to Smart'. They could have just four genders, with one for each combination, or more, where each gender is a different amount of 'Domestic' and 'Smart'.
