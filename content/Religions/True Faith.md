The True Faith, is a henotheistic religion based on the *Nesa Hakluna* and the songs of the Voices. Faithful believe that the world itself is the body of one 'Total-God', cast into a two-thousand-year stupor by the worship of other gods and idols, and that only through the eradication of their followers can the world be saved from destruction.

The True Faith is the single largest faith on the continent, followed by tens of millions across Three Skies, where it is the official and only permitted faith. Adherents are known as Faithful or the Virtuous. The exact practices and rituals of the de-centralised faith vary considerably region-to-region, but core tenets of evangelism and iconoclasm are broadly accepted.

Faithful believe that all gods exist, and are aspects of the Total-God, breaking off from It as life grew on the world. Most of these gods are seen as Vicious Aspects; negative thoughts and motives who tore themselves from the Total-God forcefully, and whose worship literally damages God and the world. However, they also believe in five Virtuous aspects, positive virtues of the Total-God who are destined to guide the Uswa in defending and restoring the world.

the True Faith began roughly 400 years ago in 0 OR, after a slave rebellion in the central [[Iruchu|Ruchun]] city of Oluk. Rebelling against the system of temple-bondage common in the region, five prophets known as the Voices composed the first hymns of the Nesa Hakluna, the oral saga serving as the scripture of the faith, and spread the religion rapidly throughout Iruchu.

By the time [[Three Skies]] formed in 184 OR, the True Faith was dominant in [[Prakan]] and [[Tuklua]] as well. Mainly through missionary work and sometimes through the use of force, over the next two centuries to the present, the league has used its power to proselytise the faith across the continent.
## Origin

Iruchu was once ruled by temple-lords who represented countless gods. They took sacrifice in servitude - peasant families, sometimes whole communities would give themselves up as generational slaves; 'temple-slaves'. This bondage extended even to the middle classes: merchants, gentry and warriors. Though treated better than the base labourers, they too suffered under the capricious, vindictive leadership of the temple-lords. Years of labour were given to building grand idols out of bronze, marble and stone, whilst the populace lived in mud huts.

Under such conditions, the temple-lords and their priests came to be viewed as indolent and decadent, their patron gods viewed as oppressors rather than objects of respect or worship.

Some four hundred years ago, in the city of Oluk in central Iruchu, a leading priest of the major patron god Banon, spoke out publicly against her own temple. She condemned the practice of temple-bondage and in return was burnt at the stake as a sacrifice by her peers. Outraged, the local population rose up in revolt and destroyed the temple and the priests.

As the fire consumed the bronze idol of Banon, five former temple-slaves received a vision in song, each from a different Virtuous Aspect of the Total-God, thanking them, and tasking them with saving more from corrupt, evil gods and their servants. As a choir, they relayed this song to Oluk, and legend tells that it was heard across Iruchu.

Known now as the Voices, they ruled Oluk and led armies of liberated slaves to conquer and convert much of Iruchu, driven to liberate the world from the Vicious Aspects. Thus began the current world era for scholars of the Faith, 'OR', or Of the Reclamation.

## Spread

The religion grew slowly at first going from town to town, spread by missionaries and Faithful from Oluk. but eventually, many in the military castes of their cities saw it as an opportunity to cast down their priestly overlords, and it gained traction in Iruchu.

One story from the early days of the True Faith tells of Captain Gemut and the Golden Legion. Gemut had been dispatched by the realm of the Fire-God Paje, the largest of the temple-realms. Gemut ambushed the army of the Voices, and prepared to execute them, but could not raise his blade, overcome by their song. Pledging himself to the New Faith, he returned to his masters and slew them, melting down the idol of Paje and claiming its realm for the Faith.

Spreading too via peaceful means, missionaries carried the True Faith to Tuklua and Prakan via the waterways. Though temple-bondage was not so common there, it spread well amongst the common folk, and the elite merchants of Prakan saw it as a way to weaken the powerful priestly class that sapped their wealth through tithes. Conversion was slower in Tuklua, where local ancestor gods were respected, and even to this day, the Faith is only nominally accepted in rural areas. Political upheval, and their consumption of Prakanch culture saw the Tuklut elites gradually accept the True Faith, syncretising it with their own faith and passing it down onto their people.

## Beliefs

##### Henotheism

The True Faith is a 'henotheistic' faith, meaning followers worship a single, supreme god but do not deny the existence and power of other deities, though it does not deem these worthy of worship.

Faithful believe that all gods are aspects of the Total-God, whose body is the physical world. Five of these are the Virtuous Aspects, which the Total-God released freely to defend Its body during its slumber. They guide the Uswa through song and vision, and are worthy objects of worship; they are entirely loyal to their originator, and grant their energy back to It.

All other gods are Vicious Aspects; evil or at least manipulative beings born of the Total-God's struggles and flaws. Each and every one wounded It when they tore themselves away, and worship of them is said to literally damage the world, tainting it with vice and sin and manifesting as natural disasters and diseases.

Certain schools of thought within the Faith believe the Vicious Aspects were purposely released by the Total-God so that they could be confronted and destroyed by the Uswa as a test, rather than festering within the world, but all agree that they must be eradicated. Should the Vicious Aspects prevail, it is believed they will eventually kill the Total-God and destroy the world.

Meanwhile, should the whole world be converted to the True Faith, then their collective worship will awaken the Total-God, who will reward the Uswa for defeating Its darker aspects by ushering in a utopia of plenty and new life.

##### The Virtuous Aspects

The Virtuous Aspects are seen as something between demi-gods and saints. Each represents one key virtue that Faithful strive to fulfill:

- **Eril**, Aspect of Industry (they)
- **Utun**, Aspect of Duty (she)
- **Kapar**, Aspect of Unity (he)
- **Pakta**, Aspect of Love (he)
- **Ullan**, Aspect of Courage (she)

##### Life as Movement

Founded in old Ruchun philosophy, followers of the Faith believe the world is made from motes, which gathered together in the form of the Total-God, who then created meaning and purpose from them - before they were just ash and dust swirling in nothingness. Consciousness, the soul, is seen as movement and change of these motes within the body. When the motes are completely still, there is no life, until the motes are made to move again in some other form. Movement and energy can transfer aspects of one set of motes to another.

All life is said to originate from the movement of the Total-God. As life is movement, all life that exists is echoes or ripples of Its movement in what is known as the Great Chain. As the Total-God slumbers, there has not been new movement put into the world for thousands of years; the Virtuous and Vicious Aspects are merely echoes themselves.

Worship is seen as returning some of this movement into the gods, with higher beings like the Uswa producing and returning more of this movement. The Vicious Aspects keep the energy gained from worship to themselves, whilst the Virtuous Aspects return it to the earth, where it slowly empowers the Total-God.

> The name for the Uswa species in Siychur is derived from 'those that echo'.

Because of their connection to energy and movement, fire and flowing water are common symbols of the True Faith.

##### Power in Blood

Blood is seen as the essence of any living being, the vessel for their motes movement and energy. At the same time, killing a worshipper of the Virtuous Aspects, a Faithful, is frowned upon - they will do more good for the Total-God alive than dead.

In practice, this means many followers of the True Faith use only blunt weapons against the faithful, beating them into submission, whilst exclusively using bladed weapons against the followers of other gods. Spilling the blood of the Vicious (a term for followers of other gods), is seen as returning their movement to the world and empowering the Total-God.

## Scripture

Though dedicated followers of the Faith have produced supplemental books and documents detailing the beliefs of their religion, these are seen more as scholarly works. The main scripture of the True Faith, that contains its history, beliefs, commandments and structure is actually preserved orally as a song known as the Nesa Hakluna.

First created by the Voices in Oluk, it first lasted only fifteen minutes, covering the basic tenets of the faith, and has since expanded to a three-hour long epic. Though it is nominally maintained by councils of priests in Iruchu, all but the core initial hymns are commonly changed to fit the situation and context; there is no one official version used everywhere.

The song is broken up into concerts, hymns and verses, and details all aspects of the religion. It is sometimes stored on metal and paper, but it is supposed to be sung; reading it silently is taboo. The act of singing it is supposed to return movement to the Total-God.

## Practices

the True Faith is followed by tens of millions across a vast stretch of the world, and as such, its practices and rituals are legion. There cannot be said to be a single mode of worship or lifestyle for followers of the religion - it would be easier to look at how each culture practices the True Faith as a philosophy.

That being said, there are a few themes that are consistent among all the Virtuous.

##### Evangelism

Worship of the Vicious Aspects, dark elements of the Total-God that split off from it, is said to literally damage the world, manifesting as natural disasters and diseases. As such, the Virtuous see evangelism as one of the most noble acts, because it not only saves individuals from the manipulations of the Vicious Aspects, but also saves the world itself. Missionary work, though not mandatory, is prized amongst the faithful, whilst Faithful rulers have good cause to conquer those who resist conversion.

##### Offerings

Harkening back to the days of the temple-priests, offerings of wealth to the Virtuous Aspects or the Total-God are seen as wasteful and scorned. It is usually preferred for offerings to be given in song, blood or labour.

##### Iconoclasm

Though not universally applied, icons of the Virtuous Aspects or the Total-God are frowned upon, because idols and representations can take on their own identity - it is a common belief they can be corrupted and become Vicious Aspects, grotesque mirrors of the Virtuous Aspects they depict.

##### Song

Music and song sit at the heart of the Faith, and are the main way most express their religion. This takes many forms across the world, but commonly, Faithful will sing or hum a hymn to one of the Virtuous Aspects at important points during the day, such as before eating, working or sleeping. In the traditional heart of the faith in Iruchu, a capella and wind instruments like flutes and horns are preferred over percussion, as religious importance is given to the breath used.

##### Pilgrimage

Though not required by the religion, many faithful will travel as pilgrims to Oluk, where the faith originated. There is a route around Oluk known as the Virtuous Path lined with porphyry rocks carved with verses of the Nesa Hakluna, with holes that whistle a tune in the wind.

## Places of Worship

Larger towns will have a temple, but more often a simple hall is set aside, with good acoustics - a 'songhouse'. By design, these are not grandiose. Instead they are usually homely, filled with places to sit and reflect, as well as abstract art and masonry. These places are used for group prayer and song, and in some places community meetings at the start of the workday.

## Structure

The faith is somewhat decentralised, with regions able to select their own priests and faith leaders. What central authority there is exist primarily to protect holy sites, support missionary work, and maintain the Nesa Hakluna.

##### Guides

Every community of more than ten families is supposed to select five members in good standing to serve as their Guides. Each one should champion one of the virtues, and is usually a professional of some kind.

In this, there is a sort of lay priesthood.

##### Voices

There is a central council of five senior scholars of the Faith called Voices, based in Iruchu. Though their decisions carry significant weight across the Virtuous, they actually have little direct power.

They have three functions:

1. Help expand the faith by coordinating missionary work, through a series of outposts, waystations and monasteries across the world. Missionaries also support communities in selecting their Guides, and resolve local religious disputes.
2. Maintain the Nesa Hakluna, memorising it word-for-word and having scribes record hard copies. They do keep a single 'official' copy but attempts made to standardise this have all failed.
3. Protect the city of Oluk and other holy sites.

Voices are selected by an assembly of the Virtuous every fifteen years, though by-elections are held if a member dies or is otherwise removed.

Each Voice represents one of the Virtuous Aspects, and has a retinue of clerks, scribes and warriors to enact their will.

Officially, the Voices are given the honour of serving as the Guides of Three Skies' capital, Anwakri, however they are rarely all there at once.
