
Siychur, or the New Tongue, is the lingua franca of the Three Skies League. Evolving originally from Prak, the language spoken in Prakan and Iruchu, it began in the river cities of Iruchu and Prakan, until the growth of the League saw it spread widely across the continent. It is a tangible symbol of Three Skies' authority over its vassals, where it is enforced as the one language for official business, while its wide adoption gives it utility in trade, meaning it can be heard in markets all over the world. It is said that when a merchant buys something, they start a sentence that is finished several months and a thousand leagues later, and the words is spoken in the New Tongue.

Despite its reach, it is only in Prakan and Iruchu where the language is spoken solely and natively. Even in Tuklua, Tuklut is fiercely defended as the language for official and religious matters, though it does share some similarities with old Prak. Most others in the provinces and client states of the empire learn it only for practical reasons, otherwise keeping to their mother tongue.

Some do attempt to force the matter. As an evolution of the language spoken by the founders of the faith, it also carries a religious significance, and more radical believers hold that it is the only godly language. Missionaries sent out to convert pagans in Three Skies' client states commonly teach the New Tongue as well, in rapidly erected temporary schoolhouses. It is not unheard of these missionaries to admonish or even punish children for speaking their native language.

There are many regional dialects of the language.

## Phonology

The language has four vowel sounds:

| ɑ   | a   | i   | u   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| aah | ahh | eee | ooo |

Due to the low number of vowels in the language, the meaning of words can be changed by holding each vowel for longer. These long vowels are represented by a macron. For example: ‘Sap’ would be read ‘sapp’, but ‘sāp’ would be read ‘saap’, and carry a different meaning.

The language does not make use of dipthongs, or multiple vowels together in a sentence. Words may only have one vowel between consonants.

The voiceless labial-velar fricative is an exception, which often forms a ‘hua’ sound.

## Rules

##### Syllables

- The onset can be one or two letters.
- As there are no diphthongs, the rhyme may only be one letter. It can however be extended with long vowels.
- Words can end with a rhyme.

##### Stress

- Stress is fixed, and falls on the first syllable of each word.
- Tone changes and affixes are instead used to change the meaning of words and convey things like sarcasm, questions, and so on.

##### Orthographic Depth

- This depends heavily on the writing system used, see: [writing system](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18yw1Hwb_r-vZmUDHTJNf2sypee_UF2HCyaV2GrZPC_A/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.kn6jng80h3e1).

##### Word Order

- The language uses a Subject, Object, Verb system. 
- Affixes can be appended to the start or end of words to change their meaning.

| Assigned at maturity, and reflects the character of the person.<br><br>Followed by a dash before the birth name, | Assigned at birth, and reflects the circumstances of the person’s birth. | Assigned after the first year of life. Quality [1] and birth [2] names can be represented in English, but given names [3] must always be written in the language itself.

Quality [1] and birth [2] names are separated by a dash.

It is uncommon for ‘family’ names to be represented in an individual’s name, due to the reproductive practices of the species. This being said, certain units and even other organisations may have their members take on their name as an appellation, after the given name.
