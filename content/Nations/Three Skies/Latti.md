##### ***Insular Miners***

***Pempi Lathwa*, or the Territory of Latti,** is one of [[Three Skies]]' vassals. Once slaves of the old Kiwan Empire, they are a stubborn, insular people who supply the empire with stone and ore. In the last century, some Oymaw sea peoples have settled their lands, and a festering distrust lingers between the two, born of early atrocities.

## Origins

The Lath believe they were carved from the chalky stone of their homeland thousands of years ago, by a god bereft after the loss of their child. When this god finished this great work, they wept, knowing nothing could replace their child. Their tears brought this legion of stone figures to life, and the god became known as the Weeper.

Beloved by their god, the Lath lived in harmony for centuries, until the Weeper was betrayed and cast down by their sister, Upok. Their lands cast into plague and famine, half of the Latti fled east to find a new home and a way to restore the Weeper to life. Upok bound those Latti that remained to carve into the earth for eternity, seeking a gem called Edaf that will secure her power.

Centuries ago, Latti lands came under the control of the Kiwan Empire, who enslaved them, building great cities with the stone they quarried. Knowing nothing else, the culture of the Latti formed around their labours.

After Three Skies subjugated the remnants of the Kiwans in the early 200s OR, their allies, the Tipjat, were rewarded with the Latti as their vassals.

##### Conflict with Oymaw

A defining event in the history of Latti is the arrival of the Wanmaw, or Sea Peoples, over a century ago.

Striking a deal with the three largest tribes of Wanmaw, Three Skies settled them across the empire. The Oymaw, led by by Oppou, were granted lands in the valleys of Lath. They were ill-liked; seen as heathens unwilling or unable to join the Lath in their labours.

The Lath banished them from their villages, and banned contact. When the leader of Fikh, the largest Lath town passed away, her radical daughter Rohe blamed the Oymaw, leading a mob that exterminated dozens of their people and killing Oppou.

Krintuk, the elite unit of Wanmaw mercenaries employed by Three Skies, heard of this massacre. Some lost family in the bloodbath, and many travelled the rivers east to Latti for revenge. They killed Rohe and burned down much of Fikh, butchering hundreds.

The Tipjat, now in a civil war of their own, rushed to the defence of their vassal, causing more carnage.

##### Three Skies

Today, Latti is a territory of Three Skies.

After their River Fleet brought an end to the Krintuk War, Three Skies took direct control over Latti, installing a *Mura*, or governor, and constructing several Ayma, or fortified river outposts, to maintain their grip over the territory.

The small Tolkan garrison is all that stops further bloodshed between the Lath and Oymaw, who have a century-old score to settle.  
 

## Geography

Latti is found in the the north-east of Three Skies, in a warm chaparral zone around the Arili River. To the west, the Batanki River marks its border with the [[Ahwat]], whilst highlands mark the border with the Golden Plains region and [[Etetipawan|the Etetipawan]]. Across rough highlands, [[Toemo]] and [[Sila]] flank Latti from the east, whilst in the north, various nomadic pagan tribes like the Skira and Hueban occupy the Batanki and Arilli river valleys.

In western Latti there is the Weeping Lake, said to contain the tears of their god.

Three Skies maintains several Ayma along the Arili. One of them looms over the largest settlement, Fikh, and serves as the regional capital.

Most Lath live in small villages and towns, built around mines and quarries, with farmland nearby.

## Leadership

The Lath were for centuries enslaved by the Kiwans, and their society echoes this. Forewomen called *Hekka* were assigned to run mines, handling all of the day-to-day management that bored their overlords. When the grip of the Kiwans loosened, these Hekka became hereditary positions, akin to tribal chiefs.

Nowadays, the Lath are ruled by a Three Skies governor, or Mura, controlling their Ayma river fortresses and a small garrison. The Hekka report directly to the governor, but the system below them has changed little, if at all.

Hekka have a dedicated second, usually their partner, who will serve in their stead when they are away. They have the final say on all legal, religious and labour disputes.

Though the Mura may choose to dismiss any of the Hekka, they rarely do for fear of upsetting the delicate balance of the region and disrupting ore and stone shipments.

The Oymaw have no independent leadership, as most live in the Aymas under direct rule by Three Skies.

## Religion  
  
The Lath follow the faith of the Weeping. They believe in two gods, the Weeper, who created them, and Upok, the Weeper's sister who is their slave master, forcing them to mine the earth for whichever tyrant commands them.

There is a prophecy that in the next hundred years, their god the Weeper will return with the Free Lath who fled east centuries ago. They will be freed from their obligation to Upok, and their labours will be rewarded with paradise.

Labour is their only prayer, and the Lath ritually wash and recant a hymn before every work day.

The Oymaw have mostly converted to the True Faith.

## Culture  

##### Values

Above all else, Lath value hard manual work (even if it serves no purpose), honesty, and adherence to traditions.

##### Gender

The balance of genders in Latti is very uneven. In each settlement, only the Hekka is a Dam, whilst most families are led by a Kora. Tous make up the majority of the population, and only change genders upon marriage.

##### Dress & Appearance

The Lath tend to have pale eyes and warm grey skin, like the wet chalk their tales say they were made of. Those in the mining towns wear clothes from canvas, from flax farms along the river and in the Etetipawan, which are usually undyed.

Under a hooded coat, they wear long, sturdy trousers, instead of the more common tunic. These are reinforced with brass bolts and knee-pads, and commonly worn with a thick, half-foot wide body belt, which supports the body and provides some protection.

The most iconic clothing of the Lath is their brass helm, which has a brim only at the front and the back, with a squat top. Originally worn for mining, it has become a daily wear item.

The Oymaw dress like the other Wanmaw peoples.

##### Customs

There is a heavy drinking culture, with people taking a light drink of beer before and after each work shift. Drinking houses have arisen as a way to unwind after their brutal work days, and are the main public gathering space out of which each village's Hekka operates.

## Economy

Latti is a simple economy built around mining and subsistence farming. They send most of what they mine downriver towards the Etetipawan and [[Prakan]].

Aside from this, they are known for making beer.

## Relations  
  
The Lath are a client people to Three Skies. They view them with suspicion, but largely tolerate them so long as their way of life is respected.

Owing to their long history of servitude, the Lath hold contempt for the Kiwans.

They hold a deep and abiding hatred for the Oymaw, who they see as foreign invaders who burned their lands and killed their people, a century-long grudge that shows no signs of abating.
