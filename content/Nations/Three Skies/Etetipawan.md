##### ***Land of Two Peoples***

***Tawka it'Etepi*, or the Confederation of the Etetipawan**, Etipa for short, is one of [[Three Skies]]' vassals. It is a vast collection of cities and small realms in the verdant central basin of the continent, ruled by a fusion culture of their allies, the once-nomadic Tipjat, and their old rivals, the proud Kiwans.

## Origins

The Kiwans and the Tipjat are two peoples that have been bound together by history into one whole, the Etetipawan.

##### The Kiwans

The Kiwans settled in the flatlands, soft hills and floodplains of the continent's central basin, and for centuries had an empire stretching from the edge of the great wastes in the north, to [[Prakan]] in the west, and [[Sila]] in the east.

Legend tells that they are the last survivors from a great utopian city founded thousands of years ago by a god who wished to share the secrets of life with her people. The Kiwans to this day see themselves as the heirs of that civilisation.

Their empire was, for a time, the largest and most prosperous on the whole continent, rivalled only by [[Uwced]]. Many of their old cities are still occupied today, huts and shanty houses built around bones of their monumental towers and ziggurats.

A brutal famine and plague brought the old Kiwan Empire low, and it splintered into many small kingdoms and tribes, entering a dark age. Many centuries later, under the warlord Ekke and her daughters, they united again into a formidable kingdom, rivalling Three Skies in its early days.

##### The Tipjat

The Tipjat were once herders of *Ummu*, a species of giant bird, in the lands near the Uwced Empire, living off the beasts lean meat and feathers, raiding the Uwceg here and there.

Centuries ago, the Cataclysm of Amsyr blanketed the Tipjat's grazing lands with ash. Those that survived were beset with a painful illness that caused their skin to flake and their bowels to weaken. Some fled east, to sell themselves to the Uwceg, but most fled west.

The journey was painful, and many died, and their struggle did not end when they arrived in lands less tainted by the ash. They first tried to settle in the lands of the Ahwat, but were cast out.

Lost in a foreign land, the Tipjat turned to raiding and mercenary work, for a time employed in the armies of the warring Kiwan kingdoms, though they sought their own land.

##### Three Skies

Following the events of the Krintuk War, which saw civil war within the Etetipawan over a century ago, Three Skies took more direct control over the region.

Etepi today is a vassal state of Three Skies; a client confederation. There is no single governor, as there are many city states and realms within it, so Three Skies installs advisors and garrisons in each to ensure their will is enacted (see 'Leadership' below).

## Geography

Etipa is vast, covering a huge swath of the continent's interior. It stretches from [[Ahwat]], [[Hachua]] and [[Tuklua]] in the west, to [[Toemo]] and [[Latti]] in the east. It is bounded by the Great Bay and [[Chupash]] in the south.

It is a relatively flat land, with gentle rolling hills, light forests, and floodplains, though the north is much drier, with the Golden Plains region a rolling chaparral.

The southern coast grows more humid as it approaches the marshes of Chupash, while north and east, Latti mining towns hug the mountains.

The land is criss-crossed with rivers, making it a rich territory for farming. Three major rivers run through the territory - the Krina, Batanki and Arili. The latter two meet in the vast Lake Kikil.

In this way, the lands of the old Kiwan Empire are the crossroads of the League, linking the core nations in the west to their vassals in the east.

Though not as encrusted with great cities as Prakan, Etipa can still boast a large urban population and is much larger in size.

The Golden Plains region can be found in the distant east of the territory. Whilst officially part of the Etetipawan, their power extends only as far as the banks of the Arili river. Many tribes call the vast prairie their home, including the Hukks, Aratites and Periddo, and they pay only lip service to Three Skies.

## Leadership

The government of the Etetipawan is a fusion of Kiwan and Tipjat traditions.

The once-nomadic Tipjat follow a clan system where each clan is led by a council of elders and warriors. Ruling is seen as work fit only for the old and wise, whilst war and craft is handled by the young and vigorous, so they have a leader for each.

When they conquered the Kiwans, each clan took a city for their own, and installed diarchs; two leaders, one for war and one for peace.

When Three Skies vassalised the Etipa after the Krintuk War, they installed a governor in each city, a third partner, and the triarchy system was born.

Whilst the Kiwans are the junior partners in the Etipa, their blood and culture is now closely interwoven with the Tipjat after decades of intermarriage and cultural blending. They make up many of the functionaries and magistrates that the Tipjat use to enforce their rule, also.

Triarchs have the power to collect taxes, maintain order and enforce laws and decrees through appointed clan officials, but most triarchies of Etipa are limited to a few cities and their hinterlands, at most.

## Religion  
  
The True Faith is solidly established in the Etetipawan, and few would admit to openly following pagan religions or holding false gods. The Tipjat in particular are zealous followers of the [[True Faith]], though ancestor worship bordering on apostasy is more common among the Kiwans, who leave offerings to their ancestors and sometimes monuments in the ancient cities they inhabit.

##### Dress & Appearance

Though the Kiwans and Tipjat have intermarried for generations, difference in dress and appearance can still clearly be seen.

The Kiwans have a rich red skin tone that is very uncommon in this region of the world, and considered highly attractive by other nations. They have pale gold or white eyes, and dark hair, though many shave it for hygiene reasons.

Kiwans cover their skin in white chalk for formal occasions, believing that the stones used by the Great Ones to build their cities are sacred, and offer health benefits. Even Tipjat have been known to follow the practice.

Fixated on hygiene, the Kiwans normally wear plain white and grey, to match the chalky stone of their cities. Wealthier Kiwans display wealth through fine tailoring and longer cuts, rather than through materials or patternwork.

Conversely, the Tipjat have a more desaturated grey skin tone, owing to their origins in the east and Uwced. Some have a faint green hue. They have dark grey hair and dark, blue or light grey eyes.

Tipjat practice scarification, carving patterns into their skin for military victories and important events in their lives. This is done at public ceremonies. The Kiwans view this as impure - damaging the gift of their bodies given by their ancestors - so it has not caught on amongst them, even now.

High-ranking Tipjat wear featherwork cloaks and hats, a tradition from pre-migration times. These are a major status symbol, because the Ummu herds they brought west with them have largely died out, so the feathers need to be imported from the east, at great cost.

##### Customs

Kiwans have an abiding respect for their ancestors, who they call the Great Ones. Their society is anchored around their myths and beliefs about how their predecessors built grand cities of stone and a vast empire.

In an almost religious way, they try to replicate how they think the Great Ones lived, dressed and ate, deeply valuing cleanliness and order. They have a long history with perfumes and incense, as scent is considered the main social marker.

Conversely, the tribal traditions of the Tipjat survive, notably customs around dance and slinging. All Tipjat who can hold a sling train regularly and in public, and competitions are major social events. An elaborate line dance called the *Okkamu* is performed at parties, funerals and marriages.

##### Structure

Tipjat society is built around the clan system, which is hereditary. People can be adopted into clans. Within this system, the Kiwans are seen as neither clanned nor clanless, but instead as vassals of the clan, which grants them certain rights and protections.

Kiwans associate closely with the city of their birth, and take great pride in its landmarks and public spaces. Being involved in rebuilding or maintaining the city is a mark of prestige, and will carry prized honorific roles and titles.

There is some fusion between the two, with Kiwans being adopted into clans and Tipjat taking on honoured city-roles.

## Economy

The Etetipawan is a vast land of many regions, producing a wide range of foods, goods and wares. Craftspeople in the cities are known to produce decent-quality tools and weapons, with a reputation for brasswork. The lands beyond produce much flax and cotton for clothing, whilst orchards in the south export rich fruits across the whole empire.

Iwa Okon, a city in Etipa, is known for producing delicious sweets and hard candies.

## Relations  
  
The Tipjat maintain a reputation as fierce warriors across the empire, whilst the storied history of the Kiwans earns them much prestige even to this day. Both regard Three Skies with some suspicion as their overlords, but broadly remain loyal.
