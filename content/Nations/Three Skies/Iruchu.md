##### ***Western Zealots***

***Daga-Limanat Ruchun*, or the Holy Kingdom of Iruchu**, is a nation in the far west of the continent, located in the temperate plains east of the Ajut Mountains. It is one of the founders of the [[Three Skies|Three Skies League]], alongside [[Tuklua]] and [[Prakan]], and the birthplace of the [[True Faith]]. Ruchun are the ideological and diplomatic core of the alliance, wielding great power through their command of the faith and producing scores of talented scholars and scribes through their renowned universities.

United in a single kingdom and devout in their faith, the Ruchun are the most monolithic of Three Skies' founders. They produce scores of zealous missionaries and teachers who travel far and wide, spreading the True Faith and the [[Siychur, the New Tongue|Siychur tongue]] across the empire.

## Origins

The Ruchun have countless myths about their origin. One is that they were shaped from the mud along the banks of their Tamak River by their old gods, into forms that could watch over them whilst they slumbered. The Ruchun were to be their stewards. Another tale says they travelled from [[Sila]] in the east thousands of years ago, after a dispute between sister kings.

##### Three Skies

Iruchu is one of the three founding members of the Three Skies league, alongside Tuklua and Prakan. They helped form the league after defeating the invasion of La-Kha together, centuries ago.

## Geography

Iruchu is situated on the westernmost side of the continent, west of Prakan and south of Tuklua. It covers the temperate grasslands sloping down from the Ajut Mountains into a rocky coastline in the south.

Summers are warm and dry.

Like Prakan, Iruchu is reliant on its river network for transport and trade. Its largest river is the Tamak, along which its largest settlements sit. It is bounded by the Mikra River in the north and Ebi River in the south.

The first leg of the Great Canal connects the Tamak and Ukul rivers, through the city of Oluk.

The kingdom claims the highlands of the Ajut in the west, but this is in name only. They are lawless, and occupied by hostile tribes like the Tekes and Ewars that refuse taxes and openly shirk the True Faith, worshipping false gods and periodically raiding the Ruchun countryside.

## Leadership

Iruchu has a single ruler, the *Dagat*, whose title means 'Annointed'. Though nominally an absolute ruler, in practice they rely on the *Kikrimur*, a parliament of regional landowners, religious clerics and other elites, in order to govern.

The Dagat also has a separate council of advisors. These may be representatives from the Kikrimur, but are more commonly friends and key allies. She also possesses a small standing army of professional soldiers, which is complimented in times of war by elite retinues and levied fodder.

The Dagat is chosen from the line of the Restorer, who re-asserted Iruchu's independence and full membership in Three Skies decades ago. They may be any living relative deemed worthy. The Dagat draws legitimacy from their heritage; the bloodline that saved Iruchu from foreign domination.

Many landowners and elites claim descent from the original five Virtues, whose rebellion in Oluk founded the True Faith, or others who overthrew the temple-kings of old, centuries ago.

##### Influence of the Faith

Though tradition prevents them from ruling it directly, the five Voices of the Faith are based in Oluk, and have significant influence over its ruling family. They also wield considerable power in the Kikrimur, where they personally appoint one third of the representatives. These spiritual representatives are called *Enta*, or Clerics.

Each Cleric maintains a small retinue of soldiers, scribes and other followers, which they can dispatch to resolve spiritual issues in the land. When out on mission, these are known as Adjutants, and wear a distinctive sash bearing the colours of their Cleric.

##### Kabak

The Kikrimur's strength comes from its ability to realise the Dagat's will, which they do through a lean bureaucracy called the *Kabak*, staffed by skilled scribes, magistrates tax collectors, and enforcers. Beholden to the whims of the nobles, the Kabak is often seen as merely a tool for extracting tax from the population.

## Religion  
  
The True Faith originated in Iruchu. It is the very epicentre for the Faith, and where its guiding Voices are based. Oluk, where the faith began, sits along its winding river. What pagan idols there once were here have been nought but dust for generations, and the grip of the Faith is absolute.

So important is the Faith to Iruchu's people, that their communities are built around songhouses; places of worship where the locals come together in song and thought. Principles tying virtue to work began here, and people commonly sing before and after their daily labours, even in the urban guilds.

The power of the Voices can be felt most strongly here. In wider Three Skies, pagan worship and other threats to the faith are normally self-policed or handled by Faithful soldiers, but in Iruchu, the Voices and their clerics dispatch their retinues to troublesome regions directly, to ensure their people remain virtuous.

The Faith maintain a huge number of religious schools, academies and universities across the kingdom, which educate the urban population and produce scores of talented scholars and learned missionaries, who are sought after in the wider empire.

## Culture  

##### Values

As the birthplace of the Faith, personal piety and regular worship is prized. They take specifics of the *Nesa Hakluna*, the holy song, very seriously. In particular, paganism is particularly reviled by the Ruchun, due to their history of slavery under false gods centuries ago.

The misdeeds of the temple-priests in their past has left other marks on the Ruchun. Fairness and the rule of law - religious or secular - is seen as the foundation for society, and corruption in leaders is despised almost as much as apostasy.

There is a sense that hard work is a way to worship the Virtuous Aspects and the Total-God, so personal industry is valued.

All of this has created a dogmatic but highly meritocratic people, suspicious of overreach and corruption by their leaders.

##### Gender

Tolkan gender norms are based around the traditions of the Ruchun. Therefore, they can be seen as having the 'strictest' or most orthodox traditions in the empire. The Faith encourages and sometimes enforces these norms in other regions.

##### Dress & Appearance

The Ruchun have dark copper skin, though those in the distant west have a rose-gold pinkish hue. Their hair is wavy, in black, brown and grey, and they have green eyes ranging from jade to olive. They have a slightly broader build.

Upper and lower classes alike prefer airier clothes that do not stick to the skin. Amongst commoners, this means light linens worn unfastened, broad tunics and wraps, with billowy trousers. The elites prefer richer fabrics, tailored to extend out from the body and encourage airflow, or layers that flow with the wind dramatically.

Piercings are common, growing popular after the civil wars as a way to keep wealth on one's person in emergencies. Currency is even woven into clothes or fastened against skin, to this end.

Ruchun wear wide brimmed hats with decorative knotwork and a polished metal cap on the top, meant to reflect sunlight away.

##### Customs

- Each community has a shared hymn or song, which every member adds a line to when they come of age. When someone passes, their line is removed from the song. These are sung at festivals and ceremonies.
- In such a deeply musical society, roving bands of musicians are quite common. Many larger settlements have dedicated spaces for them to encamp, and they are legally protected by the Faith.
- Non-representational art is the norm in Iruchu, as figurative work is frowned upon by the iconoclastic Faith. It takes the form of geometry and knotwork.

## Economy

Ruchun wine is a massive export for the kingdom, and certain vintages are prized as far away as Uwced.

The kingdom is the main exporter of flax and cotton cloth to the rest of the empire, harvesting and processing it before shipping it upriver.

Though it is not required by the Faith, countless pilgrims visit Oluk, where it was founded, every year. They bring their gold and their gifts, enriching the land.

Owing to the high number of religious schools set up by the Faith, Iruchu has the best-educated urban population in the empire, with many universities and academies. An academy called the Hukumun Circle in particular provides scribes and advisors to rulers and elites across Three Skies.

## Relations  
  
Iruchu, Prakan and Tuklua are the three founding members of Three Skies. They are united in a centuries-old pact, and by their shared faith. That being said, tensions remain between them.

Like the Prakanch, the Ruchun view the Tuklut as fierce warriors, though they aren’t as fascinated by them. Tuklut clans once raided Ruchun lands in centuries past, and there are still tensions in border regions. The Voices of the Faith are suspicious of the almost paganistic connection Tuklut have with their ancestors.

The Ruchun view the Prakanch as self-centred and greedy, though they respect their shared heritage and similar languages, which blended together into the common tongue, Siychur.

Many in Iruchu believe they originated in Sila out east, and those with the means visit as tourists.

The Wanmaw sea peoples are detested as savages, many remembering tales of their wars in the east.

The [[Shusa]] are regarded fondly for their high quality goods, with crowds often gathering to witness their arrival in riverside towns.
