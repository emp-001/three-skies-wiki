##### ***Highland Farmers***

***Mak-Lamranat Tuklut,* or the High Kingdom of Tuklua**, is a nation in the west of the continent, in the foothills of the En-Chula Mountains. It is one of the founding members of the [[Three Skies]] League, alongside [[Iruchu]] and [[Prakan]], and is the military teeth of the alliance, supplying it with fierce riparines for its famous River Fleet.

Living in highlands and foothills, the Tuklut are a clan-based society with an abiding respect for their ancestors, who they regarded as deities prior to the arrival of the True Faith a couple of centuries ago. They prize consistency, honesty and are fiercely defensive of their own way of life, and as such continue to speak their native tongue and honour their ancestors in a way that borders on heretical.

In time, they have come to rely on the Prakanch for their financial and naval expertise and the Ruchun for their religious influence. Despite a somewhat haughty attitude to the rest of the world, the Tuklut understand that they would lose more than they would ever gain if their allies were removed.

## Origins

The Tuklut have existed in the valley of the Ukul river for millennia. Myth has it that their twelve patron gods emerged from their homes in the mountains of the north, to make of the foothills their personal garden. Six pairs of gods each built a palace there, along with a neutral meeting place, which later became the seven cities of the Tuklut: Plukina, Raku, Emek, Koba, Anhar, Rawkot and Kajat.

The mortal inhabitants of the valley quickly came to serve under the gods and worship them, enjoying the protection offered by their power.

The gods lived peacefully in the valley for a thousand years, until one of them, Na, felt they should return to the mountains and their old homes, abandoning the imperfect world below. At a meeting of the gods at Kujet, their meeting place, Na fell into a rage, killing their partner Ehi and taking the other gods captive.

Drawing on the power of their kin, Na brought about an era of tyranny and sinful rule, treating the mortal Tuklut like slaves and ordering them to demolish all that the gods had built, so that they may leave no trace of their time spent below. This only ended when an alliance of mortal Tuklut led by 11 heroes fought and defeated Na with the aid of one of the trapped gods named Jama.

The gods rewarded them by taking each of the alliance's leaders as their partners. To this day, they are the Twelve Ancestors, heroic ancestral heads of Tuklua's clans, or *Limi*. Meanwhile, descendants of Na's collaborators are seen as outcasts and undesirables. 

##### Three Skies

Tuklua is one of the three founding members of the Three Skies league, alongside Prakan and Iruchu. They helped form the league after defeating the invasion of La-Kha together, centuries ago.

## Geography

Tuklut kingdoms cover the entirety of the Ukul River valley in the north-west of the continent. Tuklua sits to the north of Iruchu and Prakan, and to the immediate west of [[Ahwat]]. The territory is bounded by the En-Chula Mountains to the north and west.

The terrain is a series of mountain valleys, which get steeper in the north, and separate the territory into Lower and Upper Tuklua. Hilly land gives way to shallower plains in the west and south, whilst to the east, dense forests and crags separate the territory from Alwat.

Considering the mountainous surrounds, the nation has a high population, with two large cities, Raku and Koba, and five major towns, which all sit next to rivers or huddle next to lakes. Whilst the upper hills are more sparsely populated, the lowlands are thick with terrace farms and villages. Unsurprisingly, Tuklua is less urbanised than Iruchu or Prakan.

Unlike the other nations, alongside rivers, Tuklua makes use of an extensive road network to connect its settlements, which are maintained by the state.

Though Tuklua claims control over the entire valley, there are isolated plateaus and passes where royal authority is barely felt.

## Leadership

Tuklua is split into six kingdoms, called *Lamranat* and ruled by kings, *Lamra*, who answer to the high king, the *Mak-Lamra*. 

- **Wayni**, in the north. Ruled by Clan Uwa.
- **Tilag**, in the east. Ruled by Clan Adok.
- **Rakulan**, in the west. Ruled by Clan Rakul.
- **Kamak**, in the south-west. Ruled by Clan Kama.
- **Imāga**, in the south-east. Ruled by Clan Imār
- **Kobon**, in the south, where the Mak-Lamra resides. Ruled by Clan Koba.

Each Lamra claims descent from one or two of the Twelve Ancestors, and is usually the head of their respective clan. By tradition, the Mak-Lamra is always drawn from Clan Koba, who led the rebellion against Na many centuries ago.

In the past, the Mak-Lamra had little real power, and served to mediate disputes between the Lamra. However, in the past century, as the capital has grown due to its connection to the riverways and Three Skies, the high king has consolidated power and increasingly holds sway over the lesser kings.

##### Clan Administration

The Lamra rely on a network of clan-officials to gather tribute, enforce decrees, gather soldiers, and build infrastructure, often delegating to members of their extended family or friends. In urban areas, clans may have a whole network of professional scribes, soldiers and magistrates at their disposal, but in the countryside they may rely instead on whoever they can wrangle up with enough gumption.

##### Mock Wars

It is a cultural norm amongst the Tuklut to view the Lamra and Mak-Lamra as infallible arbiters, with a direct blood connection to the Twelve Ancestors.  As such, outright civil wars and rebellions are uncommon, and localised.

To help resolve disputes and reduce tension between clans, 'Mock Wars' are periodically held. These are full military exercises where troops are levied, armed and organised, with the sole objective of taking some important ceremonial item from a rival. These are monitored by clan elders, and are meant to be bloodless - participants may only use blunt weapons, and avoid seriously injuring their opponents.

As a welcome side effect, the Tuklut are always prepared for a real war.

## Religion  
  
The majority faith in Tuklua has been the [[True Faith]] since before the creation of Three Skies, almost two centuries ago. It is the religion officially practiced by all the Lamra and clan heads, and the practice of pagan faiths is illegal, though this is loosely enforced.

That being said, the cultural grip of the True Faith is not as firm as in other regions. Many Tuklut retain a fierce reverence for the Twelve Ancestors, worshipping them in private or otherwise treating them as saint-like figures within the faith.

This is an unspoken compromise tacitly sponsored by the ruling class, though it has drawn the ire of more orthodox clergy in Iruchu, who regard the practice of ancestor worship as thinly-veiled paganism, a grave threat to the spiritual health of the world.

## Culture  
  
This initial paragraph should give an overview of the nation's values and material culture, before exploring these in greater detail below.

##### Values

The Tuklut are a hardy people, and in others generally respect those they see as reliable. The four things they value most in others are:

- Courage in the face of danger
- Honesty and integrity
- Devotion to family and clan
- Reciprocity

Conversely, they condemn:

- Laziness
- Theft, which is sometimes punished more harshly than murder
- Being unreliable or inconsistent
- Overt displays of wealth and impractical dress

All of this is not to say they are dour. They are seen as loud, direct and warm in conversation, and are quick to both compliment and criticise.

Construction of buildings and infrastructure is seen as the ultimate representation of these values, so builders and architects are highly respected.

External conflict and conflict is seen as just when it brings prosperity or security to ones clan.

As the world changes and foreign influences arrive in the nation, there is a growing sense of the identity amongst the 'clanned' Tuklut versus the 'unclanned' outsiders, even as the kingdom itself grows closer with Three Skies. This is more prominent in the countryside than the cities.

##### Structure

Tuklut society is held together by the clan system, with each clan claiming ancestry from one of the Twelve Ancestors. There are many sub-clans and cadet branches, and in many places clans have intermarried and blended together.

In the cities, guilds which organise the labour of craftspeople are also clan-based, often accepting only members of specific clans and building a reputation for one kind of work.

Outside of the clan system, there are also communities of the unclanned. These roving bands and small settlements consist of those rejected by their clans, foreigners and other clanless. They are still put to work by the nobility but commonly face limited opportunities and contempt from locals.

##### Gender

The Tuklut follow the gender norms common in the western side of the continent, though they allow for greater freedom in changing gender, when families or clans become unbalanced.

##### Dress & Appearance

Owing to the lower oxidation in the taller climes of their mountainous valleys, the Tuklut normally have brighter copper skin than other nations, with straight brown hair that is normally cropped short. Their eyes are normally shades of grey, and they are slightly shorter than their neighbours.

Tuklut dress relatively plainly, seeing jewellery as impractical, but they do wear items to signify their clan affiliation. Most people wear some kind of half-body woolen cloak, stopping at the waist, with embroidered patterns representing their clan. Cloak length indicates one's prominence within the clan, with clan heads having feet-length cloaks. These are held together with a simple varnished wooden brooch. Owing to the cooler climate, these are typically worn under thick tunics with trousers.

Due to the limited dyes available in the highlands, white, red and blue are common clothing colours.

Tattoos are common adornments, and depending on clan, they are used to represent age, victories, wealth, children and many other things.

The aesthetic style of the nation's buildings is normally blocky and geometric.

##### Customs

Tuklut children generally start working at a young age. Coming of age ceremonies vary greatly between clans, but generally involve a vast party and some kind of task which the youth must complete, which is then celebrated.

## Economy

A system of corvee labour is well-established in Tuklua, where labour is extracted instead of tax. Each year, every member of a family or village is required to contribute a portion of their time to some key work for the region. This may be the harvest, building or maintaining roads, or military service, and is managed by clan officials chosen by the Lamra. In return, food supplies are handed out in times of scarcity, and villages are protected from outside threats.

In cities, a similar system is followed through guilds, though crafted goods or silver can be exchanged in lieu of days of labour.

## Relations

The Tuklut see the Iruchu as rigid zealots and uncultured yokels, but hard workers and planners. In turn the Iruchu view the Tuklut as almost pagan in their attachment to their ancestors, but skilled warriors

The Prakanch are seen as meek inn appearance but intelligent and open-minded, though there is tension as many Tuklut think they are set on pushing out their own merchants. Meanwhile the Prakanch see the Tuklut as rash and rugged warriors; ideal physical specimens.  
 
The Ahwat are viewed as savage, murderous tribals responsible for killing scores of Tuklut riparines. The Ahwat see the Tuklut as extractive foreign invaders.
