##### ***Nomadic Craftspeople***

**The Shusa, or the River Folk** as they are more commonly called, are a nomadic people that travel the rivers of [[Three Skies]] in great flotillas, ever since they fled the woodland realm of [[Ahwat]]. Known widely for their skill at craft, they bounce from city to city selling their wares.

## Origins

Many centuries ago, a great cataclysm rocked the lands to the east. Roaming beasts and monstrosities of all kinds plagued the lands, and the eastern Ahwat clans bore the brunt of the chaos. Choosing to flee rather than be killed, they embarked onto the Krina River and headed south, building great houseboats and pontoon villages.

They became known as the Shusa, or River Folk. Over time, they journeyed south and west. Remaining on the river for safety, their flotilla coalesced into a floating city, called Ulupi, which soon became a hub for their wide trade network. Soon, they were noticed by the resurgent Kiwan Kingdom, newly united under a vigorous leader.

Seeking to provoke and weaken this obstacle to their westward expansion, the Kiwans constructed a dam downriver of Ulupi. Shusa scouts and traders travelling east into Kiwan lands were met with brutality, and the message was clear: submit, or be slowly destroyed. An agreement was struck, but quickly broken by the Kiwans, who killed Amah, the elderly matriarch of the Shusa. A brief war ensued, which ended in the better-organised Kiwans occupying Ulupi, scattering the Shusa to the winds.

The surviving Shusa brought tales of the invading Kiwans to Prakan, in the nascent Three Skies League. Fearful of a potential rival, Three Skies and the Shusa joined forces to push back the Kiwans, retaking Ulupi in a fierce pitched battle. The Shusa underwent a mass conversion to the [[True Faith]], to solidify this alliance. 

In response to their defeat, the Kiwans broke their dams and flooded much of the city, drowning thousands. Remnants of the city floated downriver to Anwakri, the new capital of Three Skies, then only a village. Today, it forms the Sunken District of the city and the ceremonial home of all Shusa.

> [!Did you know?]
> In Anwakri, there is a yearly tradition memorialising the re-capture of Ulupi by Three Skies and the Shusa, and its subsequent flooding. A mock naval battle is staged in a nearby cove called 'The Scraps'. The event is normally capped off by emptying huge barrels of wine over the revellers, representing the flood.

##### Three Skies

The Shusa today have a unique relationship with Three Skies, owing to their close allyship early on in the alliance's history. Despite holding no lands, they are a full member of the league, and neither a vassal nor tributary. They have a seat on the league's ruling council alongside [[Tuklua]], [[Prakan]] and [[Iruchu]], albeit as a junior partner, with all the privileges that offers.

## Geography

The Shusa do not claim any lands, aside from the Sunken District of Anwakri.

They are a diaspora, spread across Three Skies, mainly concentrated in the riverine cities of Prakan, Iruchu, and [[Etetipawan|the Etetipawan]], and travelling in flotillas of houseboats which move from city to city.

## Leadership

Aside from the common meeting place of the Sunken District, Shusa society is organised into travelling flotillas called *Ekhe*, or factories, each one specialised in a particular craft which they hone to perfection. One Ekhe may be known for producing tools, another one for weapons or clothing, and so on. They collect their materials from the cities they visit.

Each Ekhe is led by a different council of elders, chosen for their expertise in a particular craft which the flotilla specialises in. They choose the flotilla's direction of travel and mediate disputes between members. They rely on experienced craftsfolk and warriors to run and defend the flotilla, though their will doesn't spread far from their boats.

Harkening back to their days as a Ahwat clan, the Shusa have a *Kertay*, or Clan Chief, who is based in the Sunken District of Anwakri. The Kertay, nominal ruler of the District, represents the Shusa to the League, and is the final arbitrator of disputes between Ekhe. They host Lesser Moots to handle these, whilst much-rarer Great Moots are held when something threatens all Shusa.

For their service against the Kiwans long ago, they are considered full members of Three Skies, rather than vassals. As such, they have free charter to moor at whatever city they wish, though this isn't always respected.

## Religion  
  
Having travelled among Iruchu and Prakan for well over a century, most Shusa follow the True Faith. Followers of their old Ahwat gods are few and far between.

## Culture

##### Values

A defining theme of the Shusa is survival. They know their safety is reliant on remaining useful, and pride themselves on the quality of their craft. No one else can do what they do, and that is wat preserves them. Adrift without a homeland, they also value tradition. They may have to speak other tongues and worship other gods, but they can't risk forgetting their past and their culture.

##### Gender

Shusa broadly align with Tolkan gender norms, though in their society, Koras hold much more power than Dams, and typically form the bulk of elder councils.

##### Dress & Appearance

Like their cousins in Ahwat, the Shusa have pale eyes and nickel-coloured skin; silvery with a yellowish tinge and green undertones. This sets them apart from the largely copper-skinned folk of Prakan and Iruchu.

Shusa fashion varies greatly from flotilla to flotilla, but is broadly layered and practical.

Almost universally, they wear a dark undersuit made from treated fabric, a sort of rudimentary, hydrophobic drysuit. Other clothes are worn on top of this.

More conservative types will only wear over-clothes made by other Shusa, whilst others wear whatever clothes can be bartered for where they are moored.

Facial tattoos and piercings signify one's craft. Markings on the left side of the face typically represents a person's craft, whilst the right represents one's experience with it.

##### Customs

Shusa are divided into castes tied to their craft skill, and each flotilla will have a craft it is specialised in, alongside several crafts needed to maintain their home.

All begin life as Whelps, until they are taken under the wing of an Adept, who decides when they graduate to an Adept and trains them in their chosen craft. Whelps become Adepts during a special ceremony, where they are given a tool for their craft.

Most Shusa are Adepts; they are competent at their craft but have not mastered it. When a person has demonstrated mastery of a craft, judged by the Elders, they become a Master, and direct many Adepts as community leaders.

Elders are those recognised as the best in their particular craft, within the flotilla. Though anyone of sufficient skill may become an elder, by virtue of time and experience, most are older members of the flotilla.

##### Language

Most Shusa are bilingual. Amongst their people, they speak the original Ahwat language, Alwu, though after two centuries in isolation, it now sounds old fashioned to current Ahwat. They are deeply protective of this, seeing it as their final link to the old homeland.

Shusa are also among the most fluent speakers of [[Siychur, the New Tongue|Siychur]], having travelled for so long among Three Skies' core. Their accent is said to be well-enunciated.

## Economy

The Shusa are a nomadic people who live on mobile Ekhe factory-flotillas. They travel from city to city, trading raw goods and food for crafted items like furniture, weapons, tools, boats, clothes and other finished goods. Each Ekhe has a different specialism.

## Relations  
  
Even after centuries have passed, the Shusa still resent the Kiwans for destroying Ulupi, and they generally avoid their cities.

Connected by blood and history, the Ahwat clans are friendly to the Shusa, who they see as lost cousins. The feeling is mutual, though Shusa rarely choose to return to their homeland.

Tuklua, Iruchu and Prakan - the Big Three of the League. These are the clients, sponsors, protectors, and wardens of the Shusa. They have a different relationship with each city, but above all the River Folk are cautious around them, for any excuse could be taken to make them settle, or subjugate them, should they prove a problem.
