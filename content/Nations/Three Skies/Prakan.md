##### ***River Traders***

***Tolpunom Prakanch*, or the Prakan League of Flowers**, is one of the founding members of [[Three Skies]], alongside [[Tuklua]] and [[Iruchu]]. It is an economic powerhouse with trade ports across the continent and a vast merchant navy, which makes it the financial backbone of the alliance.

Living in lowlands and floodplains criss-crossed with rivers, the Prakanch are masters of the waterways and merchants of great renown. They prize adaptability, intelligence and their own security above all, whilst their open-minded nature makes them the glue keeping Three Skies together.

Though bowed to the [[True Faith]] of the Ruchun and reliant on the militant Tuklut for protection, they cultivate their own power as masters of the waterways and self-proclaimed cynosure for culture and progress in the world.

## Origins

Legend has it that the Prakanch arrived on land from across the sea, hailing from a city called Mido on a distant isle, where a million souls lived in marble homes with streets lined with gold. They lived for millennia, free from all want and fear, but were struck down by the gods in their hubris, their city razed by barbarians and their fallen kin, some say risen from the dead. Those who survived fled across the sea amidst a terrible storm, and landed in the delta of the great Krina River.

There, they intermixed with the ancestors of the Ruchun and their kin, and founded their own cities - swearing to never again put all their hopes into just one and tempt the wrath of the heavens. Now, every city claims descent from the residents of one of Mido's many districts, weaving supposed images of them into their monuments and banners.

##### Three Skies

Prakan is one of the three founding members of the Three Skies league, alongside Tuklua and Iruchu. They helped form the league after defeating the invasion of La-Kha together, centuries ago.  
 

## Geography

Prakan sits around the mighty Krina River, which meets the sea to the south. In the west, forests and the Tamak River mark its border with Iruchu, whilst the dense forests and rough uplands of Hachua mark its eastern border. In the south, the Krina flows into the Gulf of Song in the west and the Gulf of Flowers in the east. The confluence of the Ukul and Krina rivers mark its northernmost point.

It is the most densely populated area on the whole continent, with large tracts of fertile farmland and many cities dotting the landscape, following the river flow. No one city stands above the rest, though a handful, like Vemmu, Kake and Debaw in the Krina delta are particularly wealthy and influential. Whilst there are a few towns and villages inland, all of the largest settlements are along the rivers.

The Krina River floods periodically, and there is a complex system of dams, levees and canals to redirect the flow and irrigate the land. Prakan is one of the breadbaskets of the continent, second only to [[Chupash]].

## Leadership

The cities of Prakan are united in the League of Flowers, which formed during the wars of La-Kha in the second century OR. The League has a central Congress called the *Ekat*, with representatives drawn from and elected by the elites of each city. Every ten years, the Ekat select a council of five, the *Summa*, to serve as leaders of the League. Two of the Summa serve as Prakan's representatives in Three Skies.

All citizens with land, or an agreed-upon amount of wealth may vote, regardless of their gender or station.

The Summa may set tariffs, wage war and submit decrees across the whole League, but relies on the member cities themselves to enforce its will. Despite this, it does employ a small complement of magistrates, who act as their mouth, eyes and ears in the member cities, seeing that their mandate is carried out.

Beneath all this, each member city has its own form of government. Most are ruled by either a single wealthy magnate, an influential family, or a council of burghers.

Out in the countryside, villages and towns are typically ruled by local elders or ealdormen, who pledge an oath of loyalty and tithe to their closest city.

A densely urban society, taxation and laws are enforced by bureaucrats, local to each city. Prakan operates a hybrid corvee system, meaning urban guilders may contribute tax by silver or goods, but peasants and farmers contribute mainly their labour, alongside a grain levy.

## Religion  
  
Nestled next to the religion's heartland in Iruchu, Prakan was one of the first places to convert to the True Faith, hundreds of years ago. Though they are not as zealous as the Ruchun, scant few now would admit to worshipping other gods in their cities, and what remains of their old traditions have scattered to the winds of history.

## Culture  
 

##### Values

Prakanch values are split down urban-rural lines, but share an abhorrence for open violence, and a strict code of personal conduct. In a culture where the rivers were open to all and travel was relatively easy and quick, brazen and aggressive behaviour was shunned.

The lower classes have a strong tradition around guest right and mutual respect between strangers. Being loud and confrontational is seen as crude and avoided, and those who have broken rules are typically ostracised or banished instead of being killed or physically punished.

In the upper classes, these values manifest as a need for subtlety and self-awareness, codified as a sense of 'Virtue' based around proper etiquette and behaviour.

There is also a profound individualism among the monied city folk and merchants, who prize personal achievement and guile above all.

##### Gender

Prakanch gender norms vary little from the Tolkan norm, though the domestic Kora and Kima genders are allowed more personal mobility than among the Ruchun, particularly in the cities, where they are allowed to own property and vote.

##### Dress & Appearance

Prakanch, like most Uswa on the western side of the continent, have coppery skin with either amber or grey eyes. They are typically much taller than the Tuklut, but lack the same thick muscle. Their hair tends to be thick and unruly; wavy.

Norms of appearance are different by class.

The lower classes generally have a paler, sun-bleached copper skin tone. They wear their hair short, or braid it out of the way. Mid-length tunics are common, held at the waist with an embossed belt or patterned sash representing their family's town or village. Broadly, their clothing is practical, wearing lighter fabrics in the heat. Farmers are known for their wicker hats that resemble gabled roofs.

Owing to is production here, more commoners can be found wearing jewellery of gold and silver than elsewhere.

The fashions of the urban elites is cosmopolitan, reflecting the vast material wealth flowing into their cities from all parts of the continent. Silk, brocade, velvet and other rich fabrics are common, usually adorned with intricate gold jewellery.

Tracing back to an old [[Uwced|Uwceg]] trend, enamel in red or black is sometimes affixed or glued to the face and hands, depicting idealised forms that hide scars and imperfections. Some go further, and coat their teeth with enamel, mother-of-pearl and other precious metals. Make-up is also commonplace, though sharp lines are favoured over blending.

The elites rarely cut their hair, growing it well past the point of practicality and dying it in bright primary colours.

Upper class Prakanch have a long-standing obsession with Tuklut warriors, to the point that trousers have replaced tunics, and their patterned clan half-cloaks sell for exorbitant amounts to wealthy merchants.

##### Customs

- Water rituals are common in rural areas, even with the coming of the True Faith. Births, marriages, deaths - all are officiated in the shallows.
- Boat races and rowing are popular sports among commoners and elites.
- Festivals are held seasonally to celebrate the flooding of rivers and the harvest

## Economy

Prakan has an advanced economy built around trade, cottage industries and services. They have a primitive banking system, and extract a huge amount of wealth from their trade network, which runs almost the full length of the continent.

A large part of their economy is built around importing luxury goods from the distant provinces of the empire, and selling these on at a profit. The Prakanch also export crafted finished goods like clothes, tools and weapons, and they produce a large amount of food and sugar.

## Relations  
 

The Prakanch are a member of Three Skies, so they are close allies with Iruchu and Tuklua.

The Tuklut view the Prakanch as good with money, but vaguely unreliable and feeble, almost decadent. They see Prakanch merchants as invasive. The Prakanch think of the Tuklut as rugged and less cultured, but fierce warriors.

The Ruchun see the Prakanch as valuable missionaries of the faith, who carry its torch throughout the world via trade. That said, they also see them as decadent and self-concerned in diplomacy. The Prakanch see the Ruchun as leaders of the faith with an ancient culture and talent for diplomacy, but also as inflexible religious zealots.
